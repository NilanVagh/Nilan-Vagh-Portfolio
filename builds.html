The problem

Performance calibration at scale is a decision coordination problem. Multiple managers, multiple divisions, inconsistent rating distributions, promotion recommendations without guardrails, and no visibility into whether the process is running consistently or fairly across the organisation. Most companies run calibration via spreadsheets and calendar invites. The outcome is recorded in the HRIS. Everything in between is ungoverned.

The design decision

Build the calibration process as a governed workflow — not a meeting facilitation tool, not a reporting dashboard. Async triage before live calibration, so managers arrive prepared rather than deliberating in real time. Role-based visibility so executives see cross-divisional distribution while managers see only their cohort. Promotion guardrails that surface eligibility criteria at the point of recommendation, not after. Decision state that persists and is auditable.

The governance logic

  • Rating distribution guardrails enforce cohort-level parameters before finalisation
  • Promotion recommendations require eligibility confirmation before routing for approval
  • Role-based access controls what each participant can see and act on
  • Divisional and executive views aggregate without exposing individual manager data cross-functionally
  • Full audit trail: who changed what, when, and from what state

Why a custom build — and when it's worth it

Onyx runs two regions. The US team uses Lattice's native calibration — it fits their process. The ANZ team doesn't. Same company. Same tool available. Different answer.

ANZ calibration is more complex: a larger population relative to the region, different employment law context, stronger cultural norms around due process, and a harder challenge around managing performance consistently across a more tenured workforce. Lattice's calibration is built around the default process. ANZ's process isn't the default. No vendor configuration closes that gap — because the gap isn't in the features, it's in the context.

That said — custom building isn't always the right answer. The honest test:

Worth building when
  • The process runs on a fixed cycle and affects a large population
  • The cost of running it badly is high — comp, promotion, retention, legal exposure
  • No vendor tool captures enough organisational context without significant workarounds
  • The governance logic needs to be embedded, not bolted on
  • The organisation will use it repeatedly — not a one-time event
Probably not worth building when
  • A simple report will do — vibe-code it in an afternoon
  • The process is genuinely standard and a vendor tool fits well enough
  • The maintenance overhead exceeds the governance value
  • The complexity is in the data, not the decision logic

The Calibration Engine sits firmly in the first column. It took weeks of design thinking before a line of code was written — not because the build was technically hard, but because the governance decisions are genuinely complex. What are the exception paths? Who sees what at each stage? What happens when a manager disputes a rating in the live session? Those questions can't be answered by a vendor. They can only be answered by someone who has run this process, in this kind of organisation, and knows what breaks.

Integrations & where work happens

Design principle — governance lives in the tool

Calibration is a cyclical, live-session event. 95% of the work happens inside the tool — that's the point. The approval chain, promotion sign-off, and executive review are all governed workflow states within the Calibration Engine. The audit trail is a natural output, not a separate process.

  • Managers complete async triage in the tool before live sessions — they arrive prepared, not deliberating in real time
  • Promotion approvals routed and captured within the tool — full decision chain visible to CPO without leaving the workflow
  • Slack notification when async triage window opens — one-touch link back into the tool
  • Calibration outputs written back to Lattice performance records on cycle close
Future state — with Engineering support
  • Real-time Lattice API sync replaces manual data extract at cycle open
  • Post-calibration rating changes pushed back to Lattice automatically — removing the manual load step

The problem

Remuneration review planning is typically done in spreadsheets — budget set at the top, distributed to managers, consolidated manually, with governance existing only as a process document nobody follows consistently. The People and TR teams spend most of the cycle chasing errors, correcting outliers, and reconciling spreadsheet versions. By the time decisions reach the system of record, the real work — the budget modelling, the cohort analysis, the eligibility logic — has already happened in a format nobody can audit.

The design decision

This is not a comp admin tool. It's an upstream decision-support workbench — the place where the hard questions get answered before anything moves into the system of record. Where is pay pressure? How much budget is available after reserves? How do different budget choices land across cohorts? The tool exists to give TR and People leadership the analytical surface they need to make those decisions well, with the right guardrails built in from the start.

Explicitly not: manager-facing, an approval workflow, a payroll write-back tool, or a long-term generic platform. Cycle-specific by design. Downstream execution moves to ChartHop.

What it does

  • Budget setting — Set Rem Review budget at Group, P&L Entity, and Employing Entity level. Reserve buckets for Promotion, Retention, and Market Correction. Budget available = total minus reserves.
  • Modelling methods — % increase, dollar increase, fixed AUD pool allocation to cohort, % of available budget to cohort. Apply increases to filtered cohorts with attrition-adjusted sensitivity view.
  • Scenario management — Create, save, duplicate, reset, and compare scenarios side-by-side. Labels, one-line summaries, and a full modelling audit trail.
  • Eligibility logic — Automatic exclusion of contractors, casuals, interns, fixed-term employees. Eligibility rules based on employment type, hire date, recent salary-impact changes, and parental leave (excluded where FY26 worked days under 90).
  • Analytics cuts — P&L Entity, Employing Entity, Division, Job Category, Job Grade, Diversity — showing eligible employees, average increase, headcount share, payroll share, and spend in AUD.

Workforce Intelligence Layer

From descriptive headcount reporting to decision-ready visibility

Built forOnyx — all divisions
StackGoogle Apps Script + Sheets
Decision domainWorkforce visibility

The problem

Most headcount reporting tells you what happened — FTE by division, attrition rate, tenure distribution. It doesn't tell you what to do about it. Performance, engagement, FTE, and lifecycle data sit in separate systems, pulled manually, reported separately. By the time the report reaches a decision-maker it's already stale and the interpretation is left to the reader.

The design decision

Integrate the data sources that actually drive workforce decisions — not just headcount, but performance tier, engagement signal, flight risk, lifecycle stage — and surface them in a single decision-ready layer. The output isn't a dashboard. It's a set of prioritised signals with clear decision prompts attached.

What it demonstrates

  • Cross-source data integration without a data warehouse
  • Signal-based visibility design vs. metric-based reporting design
  • How role-based visibility works across executive, People team, and manager layers
  • The difference between a dashboard and a decision support surface

Data sources

BambooHRHeadcount snapshots, attrition events, tenure distribution, lifecycle stage, hire/exit dates
LatticePerformance tier distribution by division and manager — layered as a signal on top of headcount data
Lattice (Engagement)Quarterly engagement survey results — manually pushed after each survey cycle closes
Monday.comParental leave tracker — integrated as a workforce lifecycle signal, not just an absence record

Stakeholders involved

CPO / ExecutiveCross-divisional snapshot — headcount movement, attrition signals, performance distribution
People PartnersDivision-level signals — flight risk indicators, lifecycle events, exception flags
People OpsOwns the data integration layer, monthly refresh cycle, signal design, and distribution

How it works today

Current state
  • Monthly manual refresh — People Ops pulls from BambooHR, Lattice, and Monday.com into a consolidated data model
  • Automated email sent to stakeholders on refresh — role-based view links, no login required
  • Quarterly engagement data manually pushed after each survey cycle closes
Future state — with Engineering / Data support
  • Real-time or weekly data pipeline replaces monthly manual refresh
  • Weekly Slack digest to People Partners surfacing division-level anomalies and decision prompts
  • Flight risk scoring layer using performance + tenure + engagement signal combination

Job Levelling Tool

Structured role evaluation with governance and audit integration

Decision domainJob architecture
StackGoogle Apps Script
StatusPrototype basis exists — Zip build next

The problem

Job levelling decisions are some of the most consequential and inconsistent in any People function. Without a structured evaluation rubric, level and band recommendations vary by manager, by division, by who's in the room. The decision exists but the logic behind it is invisible — and unauditable.

The design decision

Evaluate roles against a structured rubric and output a level recommendation with the reasoning made explicit. Not a classification tool — a decision support tool that surfaces the inputs, applies the logic, and produces a recommendation the governance layer can act on. Output includes proposed salary range and anchor point — not just a level label.

What it demonstrates

  • Rubric-based evaluation as a governed decision rather than a judgment call
  • How levelling integrates into compensation band governance
  • Audit trail design for classification decisions
  • Confidence scoring and fuzzy-flag routing to People review

Data sources

Job architecture frameworkLevel criteria, scope definitions, and band boundaries — the structured logic the tool applies
Salary band dataCompensation ranges by level — surfaced at point of decision with proposed range and anchor
BambooHRExisting role titles and levels — consistency benchmarking against comparable roles
Manager input (JD)Manager submits JD or role description — tool analyses, applies rubric, outputs recommendation with confidence score

Stakeholders involved

Hiring ManagersSubmit JD or role summary — receive level, salary range, and proposed anchor via self-serve. No People Partner needed for standard cases.
People OpsArchitects the framework, reviews fuzzy-flagged edge cases, maintains job architecture governance log
Total RewardsReceives level + salary range output — confirms band alignment before offer or role change is approved
FinanceNotified when levelling decision has compensation implications affecting headcount budget

Build pathway

Current state — prototype logic built
  • Manager submits JD → tool analyses against rubric → outputs level, salary range, anchor, confidence score
  • Slack notification to People Partner when confidence is low or title flags inflation risk
  • Every decision logged: requestor, role, level, salary range, confidence, routing outcome
  • Levelling Certificate generated — attached to headcount or role change request
Three-phase roadmap
  • Phase 1: Build functional tool in Google Apps Script — validate logic with real roles
  • Phase 2: Move to an internal LLM layer — manager pastes JD, AI analyses against job architecture, produces structured recommendation with rationale. Confidence score determines auto-approve or People review.
  • Phase 3 (with Engineering): Manager submits in Slack → analysis runs → Certificate generated → logged to governance dashboard. The tool comes to where the work happens.

EOR Entity Readiness Matrix

When to exit EOR and establish a legal entity — governed, weighted, auditable

Built forOnyx — 5 EOR markets
StackGoogle Apps Script
Decision domainGlobal workforce governance
StakeholdersFinance · Legal · Business · People
Open live demo →

The problem

Onyx operates in 7 markets. Two are legal entities. Five run on EOR via Deel. As headcount grows in key markets, the question of when to convert to a legal entity becomes urgent — but it's a decision that spans Finance (Tax PE risk, break-even), Legal (compliance obligations), and Business (strategic commitment). Without a shared framework, each stakeholder makes the call from their own lens. The People team gets stuck in the middle with no defensible, auditable basis for a multi-million dollar recommendation.

The design decision

People Team cannot own this in a silo. If People dictates Tax PE risk, Finance won't trust it. If People dictates compliance triggers, Legal will override it. The right model: People Team architects the system, domain experts own their inputs. Two lanes — quantitative (automated from headcount, payroll, break-even data) and qualitative (human-assessed ratings owned by Finance, Legal, and Business). People operates the framework and holds the governance layer together.

The governance logic

  • 7 weighted exit triggers — Finance owns Tax PE (25%) and Break-Even (20%), Business owns Strategic Commitment (15%), People owns Headcount and Permanence
  • Hard Override Rule: Tax PE Risk = 3 triggers RED automatically — you cannot average away existential legal risk
  • RAG scoring: Green (stay on EOR) → Amber (monitor feasibility) → Red (initiate entity setup workstream)
  • RED status automatically triggers an escalation alert to Finance, Legal, and the CPO
  • Every input change logged with timestamp, actor role, and business reason — full audit trail

Try the demo: update UK Tax PE Risk to 3 in Tab 2 to trigger the Hard Override and see the escalation alert fire.

Data sources

DeelEOR headcount by country, contractor vs permanent split, EOR vendor fees by market
FinancePayroll spend by market, estimated entity run cost, one-time setup cost modelling
Finance / Tax (qualitative)Tax PE and business activity risk rating — owned by Finance / Tax, not People
Legal (qualitative)EOR capability gap — compliance obligations, employment law exposure, local invoicing requirements
Business leads (qualitative)Strategic market commitment rating — long-term intent, customer presence, growth trajectory

Stakeholders involved

People OpsArchitects the framework, operates the matrix, facilitates cross-functional input — does not own individual driver ratings
Finance / TaxOwns Tax PE risk rating (25% weight) and break-even financial inputs — highest-weighted driver
LegalOwns EOR capability gap rating — compliance exposure, employment law obligations
Business LeadsOwns strategic market commitment rating — long-term intent and growth signals
CPO / CFOReceive automatic escalation alert when a market hits RED — trigger for entity setup workstream

Review cadence & triggers

Quarterly review cycle
  • Finance, Legal, Business, and People review the matrix together each quarter — each stakeholder updates their owned inputs
  • RED status triggers automatic escalation email to Finance, Legal, and CPO
  • Every change logged with timestamp, actor role, and reason — full audit trail
Real-time triggers — outside the quarterly cycle
  • When EOR headcount crosses a defined threshold, an automatic flag is raised for out-of-cycle review
  • Deel API integration would pull headcount and fee data automatically — enabling real-time threshold alerts
  • Slack alert to Finance and Legal when a country crosses Amber — earlier signal, faster response
