Calibration Engine

Performance calibration as a governed decision workflow

The problem

Performance calibration at scale is a decision coordination problem. Multiple managers, multiple divisions, inconsistent rating distributions, promotion recommendations without guardrails, and no visibility into whether the process is running consistently or fairly across the organisation. Most companies run calibration via spreadsheets and calendar invites. The outcome is recorded in the HRIS. Everything in between is ungoverned.

The design decision

Build the calibration process as a governed workflow — not a meeting facilitation tool, not a reporting dashboard. Async triage before live calibration, so managers arrive prepared rather than deliberating in real time. Role-based visibility so executives see cross-divisional distribution while managers see only their cohort. Promotion guardrails that surface eligibility criteria at the point of recommendation, not after. Decision state that persists and is auditable.

The governance logic

  • Rating distribution guardrails enforce cohort-level parameters before finalisation
  • Promotion recommendations require eligibility confirmation before routing for approval
  • Role-based access controls what each participant can see and act on
  • Divisional and executive views aggregate without exposing individual manager data cross-functionally
  • Full audit trail: who changed what, when, and from what state

Why a custom build — and when it's worth it

Onyx runs two regions. The US team uses Lattice's native calibration — it fits their process well. The ANZ team doesn't. Same company. Same tool available. Different answer.

ANZ calibration is more complex: a larger population relative to the region, different employment law context, stronger cultural norms around due process, and a harder challenge around managing performance consistently across a more tenured workforce. Lattice's calibration is built for the median process. ANZ's process isn't median. No vendor configuration closes that gap — because the gap isn't in the features, it's in the context.

That said — custom building isn't always the right answer. The honest test:

Worth building when
  • The process runs on a fixed cycle and affects a large population
  • The cost of running it badly is high — comp, promotion, retention, legal exposure
  • No vendor tool can capture the local context without significant workarounds
  • The governance logic needs to be embedded, not bolted on
  • The organisation will use it repeatedly — not a one-time event
Probably not worth building when
  • A simple dashboard or report will do — vibe-code it in an afternoon
  • The process is genuinely standard and a vendor tool fits well enough
  • The maintenance overhead exceeds the governance value
  • The complexity is in the data, not the decision logic

The Calibration Engine sits firmly in the first column. It took weeks of design thinking before a line of code was written — not because the build was technically hard, but because the governance decisions are genuinely complex. What are the exception paths? Who sees what at each stage? What happens when a manager disputes a rating in the live session? Those questions can't be answered by a vendor. They can only be answered by someone who has run this process, in this organisation, and knows what breaks.

Data sources

Lattice Performance ratings, review completion status, goal scores, manager assessments
BambooHR Employee records, tenure, current level, manager hierarchy, division, and employment type
Monday.com PIP and formal warning flags — used as automatic exclusion logic for promotion eligibility
People Team input Distribution targets, calibration parameters, promotion guardrail thresholds by level

Stakeholders involved

Integrations & where work happens

Design principle — governance lives in the tool

Calibration is a cyclical, live-session event. 95% of the work happens inside the tool — that's the point. CPO and executives don't approve promotions outside the tool via email or Slack. The approval chain, promotion sign-off, and executive review are all governed workflow states within the Calibration Engine itself. The audit trail is a natural output, not a separate process.

  • Managers complete async triage in the tool before live sessions — they arrive prepared, not deliberating in real time
  • Promotion approvals routed and captured within the tool — full decision chain visible to CPO without leaving the workflow
  • Slack notification when async triage window opens — one-touch link back into the tool
  • Calibration outputs written back to Lattice performance records on cycle close
Future state — with Engineering support
  • Real-time Lattice API sync replaces manual data extract at cycle open
  • Post-calibration rating changes pushed back to Lattice automatically — removing the manual EIB load step

Planning infrastructure

Rem Review Planning Studio

Governed planning infrastructure for remuneration review

The problem

Compensation planning is typically run in spreadsheets distributed to managers, consolidated manually by People, and governed by a process document that nobody follows consistently. Individual decisions are made without visibility into cohort distribution. Budget overruns are discovered at consolidation, not prevented at allocation. The governance exists on paper. It isn't built into the tool.

The design decision

This is not proof of compensation domain expertise. It's proof of what governance-embedded planning infrastructure looks like when remuneration review is the decision domain. The tool builds planning as a pre-cycle modelling environment with governance enforced at every step — not a spreadsheet with more columns. Managers work within allocated budget envelopes. Scenario modelling shows the distribution impact of decisions before they're submitted. Cohort-level logic prevents outliers without manual review.

The governance logic

  • Budget allocation enforced at manager level — cannot exceed envelope without escalation
  • Compensation positioning analysis surfaces compa-ratio and band placement at point of decision
  • Cohort distribution logic flags outliers before submission
  • Eligibility rules applied automatically — parental leave, PIPs, recent joiners
  • Governance-ready outputs structured for Finance and Board review

Data sources

BambooHR Employee records, current base salary, level, band, tenure, employment type, and parental leave status
Monday.com PIP tracker and parental leave dates — used for automatic eligibility exclusion logic
TR Team modelling rules Budget envelopes by division, merit increase guidelines, STI parameters, compa-ratio targets
Finance Approved total compensation budget by division and level band

Stakeholders involved

Total RewardsSets modelling rules, budget parameters, eligibility criteria, and governance outputs
FinanceApproves budget envelopes, reviews final allocation against headcount plan
ManagersSubmit merit and STI recommendations within allocated envelope
People PartnersReview cohort distribution, flag outliers, manage exceptions before submission
CPO / BoardReceive governance-ready output for final approval and disclosure

Integrations & where work happens

Current state
  • Manager allocation inputs submitted via the tool — no distributed spreadsheets
  • Approved outcomes written back to BambooHR compensation records on cycle close
  • Governance summary exported in Finance-ready format for Board and audit purposes
Future state — needs Engineering / Data partnership
  • Real-time BambooHR sync replaces manual data extract at cycle open
  • Manager receives Slack notification when their allocation window opens, with direct link to their planning view
  • Finance receives automated budget variance report at submission close

Decision-support layer

Workforce Intelligence Layer

From descriptive headcount reporting to decision-ready visibility

The problem

Most headcount reporting tells you what happened — FTE by division, attrition rate, tenure distribution. It doesn't tell you what to do about it. Performance, engagement, FTE, and lifecycle data sit in separate systems, pulled manually, reported separately. By the time the report reaches a decision-maker it's already stale and the interpretation is left to the reader.

The design decision

Integrate the data sources that actually drive workforce decisions — not just headcount, but performance tier, engagement signal, flight risk, lifecycle stage — and surface them in a single decision-ready layer. The output isn't a dashboard. It's a set of prioritised signals with clear decision prompts attached.

What it demonstrates

  • Cross-source data integration without a data warehouse
  • Signal-based visibility design vs. metric-based reporting design
  • How role-based visibility works across executive, People team, and manager layers
  • The difference between a dashboard and a decision support surface

Data sources

BambooHR Headcount snapshots, attrition events, tenure distribution, lifecycle stage, and hire/exit dates — the core workforce data layer
Lattice Performance tier distribution by division and manager — layered as a signal on top of headcount data to show where high performers are concentrated or at risk
Lattice (Engagement) Quarterly engagement survey results — manually pushed into the layer after each survey cycle closes. Next signal layer being integrated.
Monday.com Parental leave tracker — integrated as a workforce lifecycle signal, not just an absence record. Surfaced alongside attrition and tenure data.

Stakeholders involved

CPO / ExecutiveCross-divisional executive snapshot — headcount movement, attrition signals, performance distribution, and FTE vs budget
People PartnersDivision-level signals — flight risk indicators, lifecycle events, and exception flags for their specific population
People OpsOwns the data integration layer, monthly refresh cycle, signal design, and distribution to stakeholders

Integrations & how it works today

Current state
  • Monthly data refresh — People Ops manually pulls from BambooHR, Lattice, and Monday.com and pushes into the consolidated data model
  • Automated email sent to stakeholders when the refresh is complete — role-based view links, no login required for recipients
  • Quarterly engagement data manually pushed after each survey cycle — currently Lattice, adaptable to other survey tools
  • People Partners access their division view directly via a shared link
Future state — with Engineering / Data support
  • Real-time or weekly data pipeline replaces monthly manual refresh
  • Weekly Slack digest to People Partners surfacing division-level anomalies and decision prompts
  • Flight risk scoring layer added using performance + tenure + engagement signal combination

Structured judgment tool

Job Levelling Tool

Structured role evaluation with governance and audit integration

The problem

Job levelling decisions are some of the most consequential and inconsistent in any People function. Without a structured evaluation rubric, level and band recommendations vary by manager, by division, by who's in the room. The decision exists but the logic behind it is invisible — and unauditable.

The design decision

Evaluate roles against a structured rubric and output a level recommendation with the reasoning made explicit. Not a classification tool — a decision support tool that surfaces the inputs, applies the logic, and produces a recommendation the governance layer can act on. The output integrates directly into approval workflows and audit records.

What it demonstrates

  • Rubric-based evaluation as a governed decision rather than a judgment call
  • How levelling integrates into compensation band governance
  • Audit trail design for classification decisions

Data sources

Job architecture framework The structured decision logic — level criteria, scope definitions, and band boundaries that the tool applies to each evaluation
Salary band data Compensation ranges by level — surfaced at point of decision so the output includes a proposed salary range and anchor point, not just a level label
BambooHR Existing role titles and levels — used to surface comparable roles for consistency benchmarking
Manager input (JD) Manager submits a JD or high-level role description — the tool analyses it, applies the rubric, and outputs a level recommendation with confidence score

Stakeholders involved

Hiring ManagersSubmit JD or role summary — receive level, salary range, and proposed anchor via self-serve tool. No People Partner needed for standard cases.
People OpsArchitects the framework, reviews fuzzy-flagged edge cases routed for human review, maintains job architecture governance log
Total RewardsReceives level + salary range output — confirms compensation band alignment before offer or role change is approved
FinanceNotified when a levelling decision has compensation implications that affect the headcount budget

Integrations & real roadmap

Current state — prototype logic built
  • Manager submits JD or role description → tool analyses against rubric → outputs level, salary range, proposed anchor, and confidence score
  • Slack notification to People Partner when confidence is low or title flags a potential inflation risk — routes to human review
  • Every decision logged: requestor, role, level output, salary range, confidence score, routing outcome
  • Levelling Certificate generated — standardised output attached to the headcount or role change request
Build pathway — three phases
  • Phase 1 (now): Build functional tool in Google Apps Script — validate logic with real roles, test with hiring managers
  • Phase 2: Move to an internal LLM layer — manager pastes a JD, AI analyses it against the job architecture framework, produces a structured level recommendation with rationale. Confidence score determines whether it auto-approves or routes to People review.
  • Phase 3 (with Engineering): Manager submits JD or role points in Slack → AI analysis runs automatically → Levelling Certificate generated → logged to governance dashboard. The tool comes to where the work happens.

Governance decision tool

EOR Entity Readiness Matrix

When to exit EOR and establish a legal entity — governed, weighted, auditable

The problem

Onyx operates in 14 countries. 12 run on EOR. As headcount grows in key markets, the question of when to convert to a legal entity becomes urgent — but it's a decision that spans Finance (Tax PE risk, break-even), Legal (compliance obligations), and Business (strategic commitment). Without a shared decision framework, each stakeholder makes the call from their own lens. The People team gets stuck in the middle with no defensible, auditable basis for a multi-million dollar recommendation.

The design decision

People Team cannot own this in a silo. If People Team dictates Tax PE risk, Finance won't trust it. If People dictates compliance triggers, Legal will override it. The right model: People Team architects the system, domain experts own their inputs. The matrix has two lanes — quantitative (automated from headcount, payroll, and break-even data) and qualitative (human-assessed ratings owned by Finance, Legal, and Business). The People Team operates the framework and holds the governance layer together.

The governance logic

  • 7 weighted exit triggers — Finance owns Tax PE (25%) and Break-Even (20%), Business owns Strategic Commitment (15%), People owns Headcount and Permanence
  • Hard Override Rule: Tax PE Risk = 3 triggers RED automatically — you cannot average away existential legal risk
  • RAG scoring: Green (stay on EOR) → Amber (monitor feasibility) → Red (initiate entity setup workstream)
  • RED status automatically triggers an escalation alert to Finance, Legal, and the CPO
  • Every input change logged with timestamp, actor role, and business reason — full audit trail for decisions that justify multi-million dollar entity setup costs

Data sources

Deel EOR headcount by country, contractor vs permanent split, EOR vendor fees by market
Finance Payroll spend by market as percentage of global, estimated entity run cost, one-time setup cost modelling
Finance / Tax (qualitative) Tax PE and business activity risk rating — assessed and owned by Finance / Tax, not People
Legal (qualitative) EOR capability gap assessment — compliance obligations, employment law exposure, local invoicing requirements
Business leads (qualitative) Strategic market commitment rating — long-term intent, customer presence, growth trajectory

Stakeholders involved

People OpsArchitects the framework, operates the matrix, facilitates cross-functional input process — does not own individual driver ratings
Finance / TaxOwns Tax PE risk rating (25% weight) and break-even financial inputs — highest-weighted driver in the matrix
LegalOwns EOR capability gap rating — compliance exposure, local invoicing, employment law obligations
Business LeadsOwns strategic market commitment rating — long-term intent and growth signals for each market
CPO / CFOReceive automatic escalation alert when a market hits RED — trigger for entity setup workstream

How it works — review cadence & triggers

Quarterly review cycle

Every quarter, Finance, Legal, Business, and People review the matrix together. Each stakeholder updates their owned inputs — Tax PE risk, strategic commitment, capability gap. Finance updates financial drivers. The composite score recalculates and the governance log captures every change with actor, role, and reason.

  • RED status automatically triggers an escalation email to Finance, Legal, and CPO with country context and next steps
  • Every driver update logged with timestamp, actor role, and business reason — full audit trail for multi-million dollar decisions
  • Governance dashboard shows real-time RAG status across all 5 EOR markets
Real-time triggers — outside the quarterly cycle
  • When EOR headcount in a market crosses a defined threshold (e.g. UK hits 12), an automatic flag is raised for out-of-cycle review — the quarterly cadence doesn't wait for something that's already crossed the line
  • Deel API integration would pull EOR headcount and fee data automatically — replacing manual quarterly input and enabling threshold alerts in real time
  • Slack alert to Finance and Legal channel when a country crosses Amber — earlier signal, faster response, before it becomes a RED emergency

Internal Moves Infrastructure

Internal mobility as governed workflow, not form-based administration

The problem

Internal mobility is typically managed through a combination of forms, email chains, and manual system updates. The approvals that need to happen — from both releasing and receiving managers, from Finance for compensation changes, from HR for system updates — are uncoordinated. Things fall through gaps. Decisions are made without the right people in the loop. The move happens but the governance didn't.

The design decision

Redesign internal mobility as a governed workflow with structured approval routing, explicit decision boundaries, and cross-system integration. The process doesn't move forward until each decision gate is cleared. The audit trail is a natural output of the workflow, not a manual record. Finance, HR, and business stakeholders have defined roles with defined information visibility.

What it demonstrates

  • Multi-stakeholder approval routing with explicit decision rights
  • Cross-system integration across HR, Finance, and business workflows
  • How governance-by-design works in a mobility context
  • Exception handling when approval chains have conflicts

Future concept

Manager Operating Portal

One-page orchestration layer, not a dashboard

The problem

Managers make workforce decisions every week — performance conversations, recognition, compensation inputs, mobility decisions, escalations — but their information is scattered across five systems, none of which surface what they actually need at the moment they need it. The result is decisions made with incomplete information, or not made at all because the friction of finding the information is too high.

The design decision

Build a single operating layer that pulls from BambooHR, Lattice, and internal tools — not to aggregate data into a dashboard, but to surface the decisions a manager has pending right now. What requires your action? What's at risk in your team? What context do you need for the conversation you're about to have? An orchestration surface, not a reporting view.

What it demonstrates

  • The difference between a data dashboard and a decision interface
  • Cross-system data integration at the manager layer
  • How human-in-the-loop design works for frontline People decisions
  • Pending action surfacing vs. passive metric display
Decision domain Manager operating layer
