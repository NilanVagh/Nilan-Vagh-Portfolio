Calibration Engine

Performance calibration as a governed decision workflow

The problem

Performance calibration at scale is a decision coordination problem. Multiple managers, multiple divisions, inconsistent rating distributions, promotion recommendations without guardrails, and no visibility into whether the process is running consistently or fairly across the organisation. Most companies run calibration via spreadsheets and calendar invites. The outcome is recorded in the HRIS. Everything in between is ungoverned.

The design decision

Build the calibration process as a governed workflow — not a meeting facilitation tool, not a reporting dashboard. Async triage before live calibration, so managers arrive prepared rather than deliberating in real time. Role-based visibility so executives see cross-divisional distribution while managers see only their cohort. Promotion guardrails that surface eligibility criteria at the point of recommendation, not after. Decision state that persists and is auditable.

The governance logic

  • Rating distribution guardrails enforce cohort-level parameters before finalisation
  • Promotion recommendations require eligibility confirmation before routing for approval
  • Role-based access controls what each participant can see and act on
  • Divisional and executive views aggregate without exposing individual manager data cross-functionally
  • Full audit trail: who changed what, when, and from what state

Rem Review Planning Studio

Governed planning infrastructure for remuneration review

The problem

Compensation planning is typically run in spreadsheets distributed to managers, consolidated manually by People, and governed by a process document that nobody follows consistently. Individual decisions are made without visibility into cohort distribution. Budget overruns are discovered at consolidation, not prevented at allocation. The governance exists on paper. It isn't built into the tool.

The design decision

This is not proof of compensation domain expertise. It's proof of what governance-embedded planning infrastructure looks like when remuneration review is the decision domain. The tool builds planning as a pre-cycle modelling environment with governance enforced at every step — not a spreadsheet with more columns. Managers work within allocated budget envelopes. Scenario modelling shows the distribution impact of decisions before they're submitted. Cohort-level logic prevents outliers without manual review.

The governance logic

  • Budget allocation enforced at manager level — cannot exceed envelope without escalation
  • Compensation positioning analysis surfaces compa-ratio and band placement at point of decision
  • Cohort distribution logic flags outliers before submission
  • Eligibility rules applied automatically — parental leave, PIPs, recent joiners
  • Governance-ready outputs structured for Finance and Board review

Workforce Intelligence Layer

From descriptive headcount reporting to decision-ready visibility

The problem

Most headcount reporting tells you what happened — FTE by division, attrition rate, tenure distribution. It doesn't tell you what to do about it. Performance, engagement, FTE, and lifecycle data sit in separate systems, pulled manually, reported separately. By the time the report reaches a decision-maker it's already stale and the interpretation is left to the reader.

The design decision

Integrate the data sources that actually drive workforce decisions — not just headcount, but performance tier, engagement signal, flight risk, lifecycle stage — and surface them in a single decision-ready layer. The output isn't a dashboard. It's a set of prioritised signals with clear decision prompts attached.

What it demonstrates

  • Cross-source data integration without a data warehouse
  • Signal-based visibility design vs. metric-based reporting design
  • How role-based visibility works across executive, People team, and manager layers
  • The difference between a dashboard and a decision support surface

Job Levelling Tool

Structured role evaluation with governance and audit integration

The problem

Job levelling decisions are some of the most consequential and inconsistent in any People function. Without a structured evaluation rubric, level and band recommendations vary by manager, by division, by who's in the room. The decision exists but the logic behind it is invisible — and unauditable.

The design decision

Evaluate roles against a structured rubric and output a level recommendation with the reasoning made explicit. Not a classification tool — a decision support tool that surfaces the inputs, applies the logic, and produces a recommendation the governance layer can act on. The output integrates directly into approval workflows and audit records.

What it demonstrates

  • Rubric-based evaluation as a governed decision rather than a judgment call
  • How levelling integrates into compensation band governance
  • Audit trail design for classification decisions

Internal Moves Infrastructure

Internal mobility as governed workflow, not form-based administration

The problem

Internal mobility is typically managed through a combination of forms, email chains, and manual system updates. The approvals that need to happen — from both releasing and receiving managers, from Finance for compensation changes, from HR for system updates — are uncoordinated. Things fall through gaps. Decisions are made without the right people in the loop. The move happens but the governance didn't.

The design decision

Redesign internal mobility as a governed workflow with structured approval routing, explicit decision boundaries, and cross-system integration. The process doesn't move forward until each decision gate is cleared. The audit trail is a natural output of the workflow, not a manual record. Finance, HR, and business stakeholders have defined roles with defined information visibility.

What it demonstrates

  • Multi-stakeholder approval routing with explicit decision rights
  • Cross-system integration across HR, Finance, and business workflows
  • How governance-by-design works in a mobility context
  • Exception handling when approval chains have conflicts
Decision domain Internal mobility

Manager Operating Portal

One-page orchestration layer, not a dashboard

The problem

Managers make workforce decisions every week — performance conversations, recognition, compensation inputs, mobility decisions, escalations — but their information is scattered across five systems, none of which surface what they actually need at the moment they need it. The result is decisions made with incomplete information, or not made at all because the friction of finding the information is too high.

The design decision

Build a single operating layer that pulls from BambooHR, Lattice, and internal tools — not to aggregate data into a dashboard, but to surface the decisions a manager has pending right now. What requires your action? What's at risk in your team? What context do you need for the conversation you're about to have? An orchestration surface, not a reporting view.

What it demonstrates

  • The difference between a data dashboard and a decision interface
  • Cross-system data integration at the manager layer
  • How human-in-the-loop design works for frontline People decisions
  • Pending action surfacing vs. passive metric display
Decision domain Manager operating layer
Status In development
