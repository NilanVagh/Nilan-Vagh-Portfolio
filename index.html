The simple argument

Most People teams do not have a tool problem.

They have a “how does this actually get done?” problem.

Who owns the decision? What rules apply? Who approves it? What happens when it is an exception? Where does the answer get recorded?

That is the decision path I design.

What I actually build

Practical tools for the messy parts of People work.

The goal is not to replace judgment. The goal is to remove the mess around judgment: unclear ownership, manual chasing, spreadsheet-heavy planning, fragmented context and decisions that are hard to explain later.

Operating philosophy

Infrastructure First. AI Second.

Before AI can help with People work, the basics need to be clear: where trusted information lives, who owns the decision, what rules apply, what counts as an exception, who approves and where humans need to stay involved.

AI is the accelerant. Domain knowledge is the architect.

Trusted information
Clear ownership
Rules and guardrails
Exceptions and approvals
Knowing where work is up to
A record of what happened
Human review where judgment matters
AI assist layer

Workflow before automation.

Automate the path only after the path is understood.

Rules before agents.

Agents need trusted sources, bounded decisions and clear stop points.

Human-in-the-loop by design.

People stay involved where judgment, risk or fairness matters.

Decision quality over dashboard volume.

Visibility is useful when it changes what leaders do next.

Governance from the start.

Permissions, approvals and auditability are not afterthoughts.

Design for the real handoff.

The work breaks where ownership, context and next steps are unclear.

Built from real People work

These are not abstract AI demos.

They come from the work People teams deal with every day: messy approvals, unclear ownership, spreadsheet-heavy planning, manager questions, exception handling, compliance risk and too much knowledge sitting in people’s heads.

My goal is simple: make complex People work easier to run, easier to explain and safer to scale.
