Roadmap

What comes next.
And why this sequence matters.

This is not a backlog of standalone ideas. It is the next sequence of infrastructure layers required to build a more complete People operating architecture.

How to read this page

The Build Lab shows what is already built and live. The Roadmap page shows what comes next, why those layers come next, and what the full stack becomes when the sequence is complete.

Why this order

The sequence is deliberate.

01
Start with governed decision domains
Calibration, remuneration planning, levelling, and global workforce governance are the places where thresholds, approvals, exception paths, and auditability matter most. That is where the infrastructure layer proves its value first.
02
Add decision-ready visibility
Once governed workflows exist, the next problem is visibility. Not dashboards for their own sake, but a clearer operating layer that surfaces signals, patterns, and the next decision pressure.
03
Extend into orchestration-heavy workflows
After the governance patterns are in place, the next layer is cross-system orchestration: workflows that branch based on context, pull in multiple stakeholders, and require tighter handoff design.
04
Expose the stack through a manager-facing operating layer
A manager portal should not come first. It should sit on top of governed workflow, decision logic, and visibility layers that already exist underneath it. The portal is the surface. The infrastructure underneath is the product.
Next layers

What comes next in the stack.

Next build

Internal Moves Infrastructure

Internal mobility as a governed workflow across fragmented systems.

Why it comes next

Internal moves sit at the intersection of approvals, compensation checks, document generation, signatures, status management, and handoffs. It is the clearest next orchestration problem after the current decision-layer foundation.

What it adds to the stack
  • Context-dependent workflow branching
  • Cross-system orchestration and handoff visibility
  • Governance for internal mobility decisions
  • Exception handling across Finance, HR, and business stakeholders
Later layer

Manager Operating Portal

A decision surface for managers, not a dashboard.

Why it comes after Internal Moves

Manager-facing workflow should be built on top of governed systems that already exist underneath. Calibration, planning, intelligence, and orchestration need to be real before a portal can become more than a thin interface.

What it adds to the stack
  • Manager-facing decision access in one place
  • Pending actions, exceptions, and approvals surfaced in context
  • A cleaner operating layer between managers and People systems
  • A future home for in-workflow AI assistance and bounded agent actions
End-state vision

What the full People Infrastructure stack looks like.

Manager operating layer
Decision interfaces, approvals, actions, and manager workflow surfaces.
Intelligence and interpretation layer
Signals, recommendations, risk flags, and decision-ready visibility.
Governance and decision layer
Thresholds, routing logic, escalation rules, auditability, and decision rights.
Workflow execution layer
Task progression, status management, cross-system handoffs, and sequencing.
Systems of record
HRIS, performance systems, payroll, ATS, and other core data stores.

The tools are not separate products. They are layers in one operating model.
